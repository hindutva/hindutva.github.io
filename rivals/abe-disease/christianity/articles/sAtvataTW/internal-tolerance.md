 Certain sects like Anabaptists and Socinians (Polish Brethren) were opposed to coercion.

Even the Donatists stood out among other early Christians insofar as they were strictly opposed to compulsion and coercion ("man is at liberty to believe or not to believe").

However, historically speaking, most of these groups were minorities with little political power.

Moreover, the Donatist Circumcellions could be quite violent and terroristic and certain Anabaptists during Jan van Leiden Münster rebellion were quite intolerant (e.g. expelling citizens who refused adult rebaptism, burning all books aside from the Bible, etc.)

In my opinion, the real change didn't come from the Anabaptists and Socinians or people like Sebastian Castellio and Desiderius Erasmus.

It came from the bloody nature of the Thirty Years' War. 

The Thirty Years' War was so bloody (4.5 to 12 million soldiers and civilians, or 12% to 20% of the oveall population of Europe a the time) that most Christians were simply tired of violent religious feuding and signed the Peace of Westphalia which allowed fo the right to private worship for religious minorities. 

It wasn't full religious freedom, but it was a major milestone towards full religious freedom.
